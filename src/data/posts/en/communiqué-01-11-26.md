# **PRESS RELEASE DATED 1 November 26**

### ***Réveil Français condemns the accusations made by the TÉR and the lack of journalistic rigour on the part of *Le Parrèsiaste*.***

In the face of repeated accusations and attempts to discredit our movement, Réveil Français wishes to set the record straight. We have so far refrained from responding to every provocation, in the hope that political debate would be restored to its rightful place. But we will not allow unverified claims to become accepted as truth simply through the force of repetition.

#### **A photograph, some allegations and a lot of speculation**

We would like to address the controversy sparked by the Tribune Écologique Radicale regarding a photograph presented as being of a member of Alix Lacroix’s team.

The TÉR chose to publish this photograph, implying that it showed a member of Réveil Français. However, the published photograph has been cropped at the neck: no face is visible and the person’s identity cannot therefore be established by readers. An unidentifiable photograph in no way proves membership of a political movement.

This clearly did not prevent the TÉR from perpetuating this association, nor did it stop the Parrèsiaste from repeating it in his article, claiming that the person in question was a member of our team.

We take issue with this approach. On what basis has this identification been put forward? What checks were carried out before presenting this claim as fact? Exposing an individual in order to win a battle of perceptions is not political debate.

We cannot accept that insinuations are turned into public accusations without verifiable evidence. Political debate deserves better than mere speculation and hasty conclusions.

#### **Le Parrèsiaste: publishing before checking?**

We are addressing *Le Parrèsiaste* directly, even though it claims to be journalistically independent.

In its article, the newspaper writes: “A logical connection, given her open support on social media and her presence in the marches.” It then goes so far as to describe the person in the photograph as “a member of Alix Lacroix’s team”.

#### **What evidence supports this claim?**

Le Parrèsiaste chose to report this information without first contacting us to verify the accuracy of this identification and to hear our side of the story. It thus adopted an interpretation put forward by the TÉR and incorporated it into its own journalistic account without giving us the opportunity to refute it prior to publication.

We do not hold it against the media for covering our movement or criticising it; that is the role of the independent press. But there is a fundamental difference between reporting an allegation and presenting it as established fact. The minimum standard of journalism is to verify one’s sources, cross-check information and interview those directly involved. **Independence does not exempt one from these requirements; on the contrary, it makes them essential.**

We therefore invite the Parrèsiaste to explain the grounds on which he bases this identification and why he did not deem it necessary to consult us before publishing.

A newspaper can be critical, controversial and independent. However, it cannot claim to uphold journalistic rigour whilst neglecting to check the facts.

#### **Our stance on caricatures**

This controversy sadly illustrates a trend that we have been criticising for several weeks: personal attacks are gradually taking precedence over a debate on policy.

The TÉR accuses us of commenting on the protests from ‘the comfort of our living rooms’. Unfortunately for them, we were there on the ground and saw the smashed shop windows, the looted shops and the prefecture’s doors being broken down. In Bordeaux, a judge from the Commercial Court alerted us to the situation on Rue Sainte-Catherine. **Whilst some were posing for the cameras with placards, we were listening to the shopkeepers.**

We have always defended the freedom to protest and drawn a distinction between the right to protest peacefully and the acts of vandalism and violence committed on the fringes of demonstrations. But those who set fire to Limoges Town Hall, a listed building, are neither protesters nor activists: they are criminals. Calling them to order is not a sign of contempt for peaceful demonstrators; it is a way of protecting them.

We reject any hierarchy amongst victims: a protester who has been attacked is a victim, a police officer who has been verbally abused is a victim, and a shopkeeper like Mr Hassan, whose shop has been destroyed, is just as much a victim. Proven cases of police violence must be investigated by the courts, and the rioters must be convicted. **The TÉR, for its part, legitimises physical violence ‘when public discourse is no longer enough’: this statement is a serious affront to the Republic.**

As regards environmental and social issues, contrary to what some people suggest, we do have proposals. In particular, we are advocating a major thermal renovation programme for secondary schools and universities, a 7-centime-per-litre reduction in excise duty on petrol and diesel, a wide-ranging electrification project, and a focus on water networks in the overseas territories.

We condemn the insults directed at our candidate, Alix Lacroix, who has been labelled a mouthpiece for “infantilising propaganda” and an enemy of the French people. Similarly, we regret the insinuations regarding our alleged trivialisation of racism and discrimination. Our manifesto and our code of ethics are available to anyone who genuinely wishes to find out about our commitments. We can even send them to you on recycled paper if that would put your mind at rest.

Finally, we are questioning the paradox of certain publications produced using artificial intelligence by movements that claim to be environmentalist. These technologies also have an environmental cost, particularly in terms of water and energy consumption. Environmentalism also means questioning the tools we use, and not just those we wish to criticise in our opponents.

#### **Responding to ideas rather than making a string of accusations**

We would like to reiterate that Réveil Français is more than willing to put its proposals up for public scrutiny against those of the TÉR. We are ready to discuss our programme, our figures, our commitments and our positions. We have no difficulty whatsoever in defending our ideas.

However, we refuse to allow unverified claims and accusations presented as facts to take the place of democratic debate.

We are simply asking that the same standards of rigour and scrutiny be applied to all movements, regardless of their political leanings.

To all those who, whatever their position on the political spectrum, remain committed to open debate, freedom of expression and intellectual honesty, we appeal: let us not allow controversy to take the place of ideas.

### **Réveil Français will no longer remain silent.**
